I

The category, honestly.

None of the five neighbors below are weak tools. Each is genuinely good at what it was built for. The seam between them, a plain box a solo operator actually runs, a secret graph rather than just secret storage, governed autonomy tied to that box's real conformance, is where none of them reach.

Coolify

Good at

Self-hosted app deployment. Push code, get a running service on your own server, with a large library of one-click templates.

The gap

It deploys and manages apps. It is not a conformance engine, does not hold a secret-to-consumer graph, and does not verify its own install stays correct over time.

Puppet / Ansible-style config management

Good at

Powerful, mature convergence: declare a state and it applies changes across a fleet to reach it. Decades of proven use managing real infrastructure.

The gap

Assumes a fleet and a team already running config management, heavier than a solo operator usually wants to stand up. It is imperative-convergent by design, where Orrery is report-only, declared.

Infisical / Doppler

Good at

Secrets management: storing credentials centrally and serving them to apps and CI, priced per identity or seat.

The gap

They store secrets. They do not map the secret-to-consumer graph or reconcile a whole box's desired state, and the pricing unit scales with the size of your infrastructure, not your team.

Spacelift

Good at

IaC orchestration: running Terraform, OpenTofu, or Pulumi at team and enterprise scale, with policy and run management on top.

The gap

Priced and scoped for team and enterprise IaC workflows. It orchestrates applies to infrastructure you already provision through code; it is not aimed at a solo operator's plain VPS.

The AI-governance lane

Good at

Governed autonomy for AI agents: policy, approvals, and audit for what an agent is allowed to do, aimed at platform teams inside larger organizations.

The gap

Enterprise-priced and built for a platform team, not tied to whether a plain VPS actually still matches what was declared for it.

III

What Orrery actually does differently.

Five choices, held on purpose, that together are the reason no row above is a substitute.

01 / report-only

It never changes your box.

The reconciler measures and reports. Every convergent tool above applies changes on your behalf; Orrery tells you where reality diverged and leaves the decision, and the change, to you.

02 / self-verifying

The install proves itself.

ess-orrery doctor confirms the installation actually stands, cold, on a blank box, then the reconciler keeps watching. Almost nothing in this space can prove it is still correctly installed a month later.

03 / the secret graph

Consumers, not just values.

Every secret mapped to the consumers that reference it, and back, confirmed by identity and never by reading a value. That graph is what makes safe rotation possible later.

04 / the pricing unit

Per operator and box band.

Never priced per machine identity or agent. The band is a ceiling on how many boxes one plan covers, not a per-server toll that climbs with your infrastructure.

05 / open-core

Auditable, because it has to be.

The core holds your secrets and reaches every box, the highest-privilege software you would run, so it ships AGPL-3.0-or-later: free to run, study, and modify, in full.
